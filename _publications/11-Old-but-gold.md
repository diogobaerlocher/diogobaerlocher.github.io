abstract: >-
    Following the discovery of gold in 1694 in Brazil, pathways were constructed to connect
    coastal settlements to mining regions in the unpopulated interior. While these pathways
    initially facilitated the creation of road towns, their influence faded by the late
    nineteenth century. With the mid-twentieth-century demographic and industrial
    transition, regions with higher historical road density experienced renewed population
    growth and greater migrant inflows. We argue that this resurgence reflects the role of
    road towns in supporting early urbanization and structural transformation. Using an
    extended Rosen-Roback-Glaeser framework, we estimate strong agglomeration spillovers,
    consistent with Brazil's spatial economy exhibiting multiple steady states and possible
    historical path dependence.
