---
title: "Climate Exposure, Agricultural Reallocation, and Slum Growth"
collection: "projects"
permalink: "/projects/climate-exposure-and-slum-growth"
coauthors:
  - name: "Benedetta Bastianelli"
    url: "https://sites.google.com/view/benedettabastianelli"
abstract: >-
    This paper asks whether climate exposure in a city's agricultural hinterland shows up in
    the quality of the city's housing. We build a panel of 433 urban centres in 40
    developing countries by aggregating IPUMS-harmonized DHS household microdata to Global
    Human Settlement Layer urban centres, and we measure exposure outside the city, in the
    surrounding cropland, from CRU moisture and temperature weighted by a fixed cropland
    mask and by distance. Comparing cities in the same country and survey round, a
    one-standard-deviation difference in the total change in hinterland exposure between
    rounds moves the urban slum share by 1.4 percentage points, through drying and through
    warming alike. The response is concentrated in building-material deprivation, the margin
    that can adjust at the frequency of migration, while water and sanitation deprivation do
    not move under the same variation. It is also concentrated in cities below 250,000
    inhabitants, where the agricultural hinterland is large relative to the urban population
    and formal housing supply is thin. Hinterland temperature rose by 0.23°C over the
    average interval between surveys, enough to offset about a quarter of the decline in
    urban slum shares over the same period.
